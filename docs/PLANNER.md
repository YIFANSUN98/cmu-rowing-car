# Planning algorithm and limits

The demand on a day is attending carpool members. Driver availability has to be explicitly true for a member of that set. Starts and shared pickups are canonical location indices; people remain separate identities for seat accounting. Matrix entries are directed and carry seconds, meters and reachability. Missing/unreachable edges remain infeasible.

1. Generate capacity-sufficient driver subsets, ordered by car count. Pools up to 14 use bounded combination enumeration; larger pools use seeded sampling. At most 64 driver subsets are retained by default.
2. Tiny days (at most eight people) exhaust passenger-to-car assignments within the budget. Larger days use three insertion orders per subset, one based on long direct journeys and two seeded orders. Each insertion evaluates the added passenger in each candidate car. Up to 48 relocation/swap steps improve each assignment. Different subsets represent driver substitutions.
3. Each car evaluates all permutations of distinct pickup stops, at most four passengers / 24 permutations. Shared-location riders use one boarding stop and multiple seats. For each ordering, schedule backwards from the buffered arrival deadline and verify readiness and earliest departure. Latest feasible departure avoids waiting; an order with a readiness violation cannot meet the fixed deadline using those estimates. Include service time and route-duration limits.
4. Keep the best assignment per driver set. Choose an efficient daily baseline by total driving seconds, preferring fewer cars on time ties, then a secondary distance/route-duration cost. The baseline is the best found, not a global optimum guarantee.
5. Combine daily alternatives with a bounded weekly beam. Partial states retain different driving-count vectors. Enforce at most each baseline day's car count and at most the configured extra driving seconds over the full-week baseline. The baseline itself is always retained as a fallback. Fairness therefore can change earlier days, rather than merely reporting greedy counts afterwards.
6. Replay all feasible days with a separate constraint checker before they reach the UI. For TomTom, compare every pickup order for each selected car as a complete route, including boarding pauses and up to two road alternatives. Apply the existing time/distance cost to feasible alternatives. Retry the chosen order at an earlier departure if it would arrive late. The legacy Google adapter rechecks individual legs. Try advancing route departure up to three checks if traffic causes lateness. If that cannot satisfy all constraints, return a timing failure with unassigned demand and no falsely complete day.

Fairness is `sum((u_d - K * a_d / sum(a))²)`: selected driver-days `u_d`, eligible available days `a_d`, total selected driver-days `K`. Drivers with no eligible days are excluded; a zero denominator gives zero targets. Counts, eligible days, minutes, baseline time and fairness score accompany the plan. After live rechecks, the reported driving minutes reflect revised estimates; the allowance baseline remains the planning matrix baseline and is identified as such.

The seed, sorted stable IDs and deterministic operation budgets reproduce assignments for the same inputs/settings/matrices. Generated timestamps and elapsed time naturally differ. The default 80,000 evaluations/day cap is independent of hardware speed. Worker termination makes cancellation immediate for CPU work. Provider requests already sent may finish/bill; their late results are ignored, and no further requests are scheduled after cancellation.

A capacity shortfall is a provable contradiction under current explicitly available drivers; unknown availability may later change that. An exhaustive tiny timing/reachability failure is distinguished from a bounded search failing to find a plan. The latter is not proof of infeasibility. Incomplete days list all unmet demand and are never labeled complete. No driver is invented and no external ride is booked.

Larger and adversarial cases can miss feasible or better plans due to subset, insertion and beam limits. Input row order does not change identity or constraints. Manual locks, persistent fairness and minimizing changes are deferred.
